Designing Famealy: a food-sharing platform

Connecting busy working parents to quality homemade dinner — and giving immigrants and stay-at-home parents a way to earn income from their cooking.

TOOLS

01 · The challenge

Working parents want healthy dinner, but have no time to cook

Working parents don't have time to cook but want healthy, homemade food for their family. They're time-sensitive — planning dinner ahead for the week and expecting it on time. Meanwhile, immigrants and stay-at-home parents are struggling to make extra income with their cooking.

The solution: a platform connecting busy families to quality home-cooked food, while giving home chefs an economic opportunity.

02 · Research

Trust is the No. 1 concern in ordering homemade food

After a brainstorming session distilled into five key research points, I ran qualitative interviews with 5 people and a survey with 12, spanning both order-side and cook-side users.

"I order Uber Eats almost every night because I really don't have time to cook. If there's a more healthy option I'd love to try, but I need to know the chef and how I can trust him/her."

— Sara, working mom
TOP CONCERNS ORDERING HOMEMADE FOOD
Trust in the chef65%
Food safety & hygiene48%
Consistency of quality35%
Delivery reliability24%
Price18%

From 5 interviews and a 12-person survey across order-side and cook-side users — trust in the chef ranked well above every other concern.

03 · Design decisions

Designed for time, health, family, trust, and chefs

Design for time-saving

A meal calendar lets parents plan ahead and balance nutrition for the week, and track delivery progress.

Design for trust

Community reviews, chef intro pages, and transparent dish info help users discover and order from chefs they trust.

Design for chefs

The home page emphasizes chefs over dishes — giving chefs a branding opportunity and simplifying delivery logistics to one chef per day.
