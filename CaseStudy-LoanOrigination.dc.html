01 · Context

The flow is the entire business

Fig, a Fairstone Bank company, offers fully digital personal loans up to $35,000 in Canada. The origination flow — 14 steps from "how much do you want to borrow?" to "your loan is booked," gated by a soft credit check at the offer and a hard check at booking — is the product. It had grown organically over time and was showing its age. My mandate was to redesign it end-to-end.

Most traffic arrives through aggregator partners, not Fig's own site — so the flow is often a user's first encounter with the Fig name, with no built-in trust or context. That shaped a second, quieter mandate: build trust for Fig within the flow itself, not just streamline it.

02 · The problem

A desktop-era form, on a mobile-only user base

The pre-qual form was a wall. One long page asked for everything at once — name, DOB, phone, email, income, SIN, address, housing costs — before showing anything in return. Over a two-week sample, 4,608 users viewed it and 986 completed it: 21.4%, meaning roughly 80% of paid and partner traffic was lost before Fig could make an offer.

It was slow, on the wrong device. Median completion time was 2m 16s with a long tail past ten minutes — yet 80–90% of users were on mobile, pinching through a desktop two-column form with no progress indicator and no way to review previous answers.

Users who did get through were confused. CX tickets clustered on a few questions: will this hurt my credit score, why did my APR change, what drives my monthly payment, can I repay early. The old offer and customization screens answered none of them — the term slider led with APR, "pre-approved" jargon went unexplained, and error states left users fearing their credit had been damaged.

03 · Research & discovery

The effort was the objection, not the loan

CX was swamped by basic questions the flow itself should have answered. Outreach calls to drop-off users, meant to recover applications, kept surfacing the same story instead: people asking whether checking their rate would hurt their credit score, whether they could repay early, and — because most arrive via aggregator partners with no prior context — who Fig even is and what its relationship to Fairstone is. Some weren't even looking for the right product; they wanted a line of credit, not an installment loan.

That reframed the brief: the aggregator-facing flow needed to build trust for Fig and set the right product expectation up front, in addition to answering the recurring credit-score, early-repayment, and rate-negotiation questions (partners like Walmart routinely have users asking to negotiate rate). Direct-to-consumer traffic, by contrast, already knows Fig from its own site — so that flow stays deliberately simpler, without the trust-building layer.

05 · Key decisions — 01

One long form becomes five focused steps

The single form became five steps — loan amount & purpose, about yourself, personal info, employment, housing — each a single column with a progress bar, back navigation, and thumb-sized inputs. Contact info moved earlier: email at step 2, phone at step 3, unlocking drop-off recovery outreach for most of the funnel that previously had none.

Every screen repeats one reassurance under the CTA: "This will not impact your credit score." The credit-check moment is explicit — the CTA becomes "Get my offer" with plain-language disclosure of the soft TransUnion inquiry.

The redesign also collects four fields the old form didn't — immigration status, purpose of loan, employment status, and highest level of education — added for risk requirements but sequenced so they never feel like a wall. Employment status and loan purpose also moved from post-offer into pre-qual, so ineligible users are declined within the first couple minutes instead of after identity verification: less sunk cost for the user, a cleaner funnel for the business.

06 · Key decisions — across the flow

Four more moments, redesigned to reduce doubt

01

An offer page that closes the loop

Most users hit this screen never having heard of Fig — aggregator traffic arrives cold, mid-decision, comparing offers. So the page carries three jobs at once. Trust: Fig is introduced as "A Fairstone Bank Company," with verified customer testimonials alongside the offer. Transparency: the offer card leads with the numbers users actually weigh — monthly payment from, guaranteed fixed APR, term — with an honest expiry date, and an FAQ pre-answers the questions flooding CX: credit-score impact, early repayment, whether the rate is negotiable, whether this is even the right product. Effort: email verification is gated here, at the moment of highest intent, with a "just a few minutes to finish" cue framing what's left. The strategic call was recognizing this page isn't a receipt — it's the pitch.

02

Customize your offer, payment-first

The old screen led with term and buried the number that actually drives the decision. The redesign makes monthly payment the hero — pinned in a callout that updates live as amount and term sliders move — with a breakdown showing amount and term set the payment, while APR is fixed at pre-qual. It also states plainly: no early-repayment penalties. This closed a trust bug where quoted APR could drift between pre-approval and customization — a top ticket driver.

03

A bank connection worth trusting

Connecting a bank account is the scariest ask in the flow. The Flinks splash was rebuilt around the two real objections — credentials never touch Fig, 500k+ monthly users trust Flinks — plus a "why do we need this?" disclosure. Completion now sits around 90%, near-ceiling for a bank-linking step: here, the job is reassurance, not persuasion.

[ bank connection splash redesign ]
04

Error states that don't punish

Previously, a failed verification or timeout showed a generic error, and users assumed the worst about their credit. The redesign leads with "your credit score is unaffected," then explains what happened and gives one clear recovery action.

Splitting the form roughly doubled pre-qual conversion — the funnel's single biggest leak. Clarifying the offer, selection, connection, and error moments removed reasons to doubt, call, or quit, on the surface where most applicants actually were. Because pre-qual conversion compounds downstream, the lift carries through to bookings, not just completions.

08 · What I learned

Trust is a conversion lever

The single highest-leverage copy in the flow is one repeated line: "This will not impact your credit score." Reassurance at the moment of anxiety outperformed any layout change.

Sequencing is design. Moving employment, purpose, email, and phone capture earlier changed business outcomes without changing a single field's UI.

CX tickets are a free research pipeline — every top theme became an in-context answer on the screen where it came up.
